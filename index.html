01
PARTS OF A SENTENCE
Subjects & Predicates
THE BASICS
Subject and Predicate
The subject tells whom or what the sentence is about. The predicate tells something about the subject.
EXAMPLE

Some residents of the desert can survive a long drought.

THE SUBJECT
DEFINITION
Simple vs. Complete Subject
The simple subject is the main word that tells whom or what the sentence is about. The complete subject is the simple subject plus all words that modify it.
EXAMPLE 1

A dog with this pedigree is usually nervous.

  • Complete subject: A dog with this pedigree
  • Simple subject: dog
EXAMPLE 2

Both of these cockatiels are for sale.

  • Complete subject: Both of these cockatiels
  • Simple subject: Both
HEADS UP: The subject can never be found in a prepositional phrase. Cross out prep phrases first.
THE PREDICATE
DEFINITION
Simple vs. Complete Predicate
The simple predicate (verb) is the main word that tells something about the subject. The complete predicate is the verb plus all words that modify or complete its meaning.
EXAMPLE 1

Spiders snare their prey in intricate webs.

  • Complete predicate: snare their prey in intricate webs
  • Simple predicate (verb): snare
EXAMPLE 2

Have been found — my keys?

  • Complete predicate: Have been found
  • Verb phrase: Have been found
HEADS UP: The predicate can never be found in a prepositional phrase either.
📌
Always cross out prepositional phrases first. They will never contain the subject or the verb.

02
PARTS OF A SENTENCE
Finding the Subject
THE METHOD
Start with the verb
To find the subject, find the verb first. Then ask who? or what? before the verb.
EXAMPLES
  • My cousin from Finland will arrive this afternoon.
  • On the other side of the brook stands a cabin.
4 RULES FOR TRICKY SUBJECTS
1
PREPOSITIONAL PHRASES
The subject is never in a prepositional phrase
Cross out all prep phrases first. The subject will be in what remains.
EXAMPLES
  • One of my cousins has visited Ghana.
  • On the top of the building is an up-to-date observatory.
2
HERE / THERE
If here or there starts a sentence, the subject follows the verb
Here and there are NEVER the subject.
EXAMPLES
  • There are your new gloves.
  • Here is my nephew.
3
QUESTIONS
In a question, the subject usually follows the verb — turn it into a statement
Rewrite the question as a statement to find the subject easily.
EXAMPLES
  • Where is your parakeet? → Your parakeet is there.
  • Did you make the team? → You did make the team.
  • Were your friends early? → Your friends were early.
  • Where did the horses cross the river? → The horses crossed the river.
4
COMMANDS / REQUESTS
In a command, the subject is the understood [you]
The subject is not stated, but it is always understood to be [you].
EXAMPLES
  • [You] Please rake the yard.
  • [You] Pick up the fallen branches.
  • Jason, [you] wash the dishes.

03
PARTS OF A SENTENCE
Compound Subjects & Verbs
DEFINITION
Compound Subject
A compound subject is two or more subjects joined by a conjunction that share the same verb.
EXAMPLES
  • Mr. Olivero and his daughter planted the garden.
  • Either Mr. Olivero or his daughter planted the garden.
DEFINITION
Compound Verb
A compound verb is two or more verbs joined by a conjunction that share the same subject.
EXAMPLES
  • At the street festival, we danced the rumba and sampled the meat pies.
  • I have written the letter and addressed the envelope, but have not gone to the post office.
📌
Both the subject and the verb may be compound at the same time.

Yesterday, Jamal and I built a box kite and painted it bright yellow and green.
Compound subject: Jamal and I  ·  Compound verb: built and painted

04
PARTS OF A SENTENCE
Subject Complements
DEFINITION
What is a subject complement?
A subject complement completes the meaning of a linking verb and identifies or modifies the subject. There are two kinds: a predicate nominative and a predicate adjective.
🔗
Subject complements are only found with linking verbs (is, are, was, were, seem, become, look, feel, appear, taste, smell, remain, grow, etc.)
PREDICATE NOMINATIVE
TYPE 1 — PN
Predicate Nominative
A predicate nominative is in the predicate and identifies the subject or refers to it. It may be a noun or a pronoun.
EXAMPLES
  • Some caterpillars become butterflies. [butterflies identifies caterpillars]
  • The winner should have been they. [they refers to winners]
  • She is the next speaker. [speaker refers to She]
NOTE: A PN may be compound: Our cats' names are Wimpy and Henry.
A PN may also precede the subject and verb: What a fine speaker you are!
PREDICATE ADJECTIVE
TYPE 2 — PA
Predicate Adjective
A predicate adjective is an adjective in the predicate that modifies the subject.
EXAMPLES
  • You look happy. [happy modifies You]
  • When she left, Norma appeared calm. [calm modifies Norma]
NOTE: A PA may be compound: The yogurt tasted sweet and creamy.
A PA may also precede the subject and verb: How silly that commercial is!
TypeWhat it doesPart of speechExample
Predicate Nominative (PN)Identifies or renames the subjectNoun or PronounLouis is a mathematician.
Predicate Adjective (PA)Describes or modifies the subjectAdjectiveCarlos has grown taller.

05
PARTS OF A SENTENCE
Direct Objects
DEFINITION
What is a direct object?
A direct object is a noun, pronoun, or word group that tells who or what receives the action of an action verb or shows the result of the action.

Ask whom? or what? after the verb to find it.
EXAMPLES
  • I took my little sister to the movie. [took whom? sister]
  • She had already seen it four times. [seen what? it]
KEY NOTES
Things to remember about direct objects
A direct object may be compound:
COMPOUND DO

The parrot said "Good morning" and "Cat free to good home."

A direct object may precede the subject and verb:
DO BEFORE SUBJECT

What remarkable tricks the illusionist performed! [performed what? tricks]

Direct objects are never found in prepositional phrases:
COMPARISON
  • Tom was driving in his car. [car is in a prep phrase — NOT a DO]
  • Tom was driving his car. [driving what? car — this IS a DO]
📌
Direct objects are only found with action verbs. If the verb is a linking verb, there is no direct object — look for a subject complement instead.

06
PARTS OF A SENTENCE
Indirect Objects
DEFINITION
What is an indirect object?
An indirect object is a noun, pronoun, or word group that tells to whom, to what, for whom, or for what the action of the verb is done.

It is located between the action verb and the direct object.
EXAMPLES
  • Meli read us her report. [read to whom? us]
  • They fed the horses some oats. [fed what some oats? the horses]
  • Juan left you this message. [left the message for whom? you]
  • Carly knitted her pet poodle a blanket. [knitted the blanket for what? her poodle]
KEY NOTES
Things to remember about indirect objects
An indirect object may be compound:
COMPOUND IO

The ski trip had given Lucia and me a wonderful vacation. [given to whom? Lucia and me]

An indirect object cannot be part of a prepositional phrase:
COMPARISON
  • Jeff wrote a note to me. [me is in "to me" — NOT an IO]
  • Jeff wrote me a note. [wrote a note to whom? me — this IS an IO]
CRITICAL: You cannot have an indirect object without a direct object. If there is only one object, it is always a direct object.
📌
Indirect objects are only found with action verbs. Order is always: verb → IO → DO.

PARTS OF A SENTENCE
Classifying Sentences by Purpose
OVERVIEW
Four types of sentences
Depending on its purpose, a sentence may be classified as declarative, imperative, interrogative, or exclamatory.
DECLARATIVE
Makes a statement. Ends with a period.
Toni Morrison won the Nobel Prize for literature in 1993.

Although we were tired after working all day, we still wanted to go dancing.
Ends with: .
IMPERATIVE
Gives a command or makes a request. Usually ends with a period. A strong command ends with an exclamation point.
Be careful.
Please open the window.
Wait!
Ends with: . or !
INTERROGATIVE
Asks a question. Ends with a question mark.
Can you speak Spanish?

Did you say that you were taking French?
Ends with: ?
EXCLAMATORY
Shows excitement or expresses strong feeling. Ends with an exclamation point.
What a beautiful day this is!

How I enjoy autumn!
Ends with: !
